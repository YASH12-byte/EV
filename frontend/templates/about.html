About the Project

Predictive Analytics for Electric Vehicle Market Growth using Hybrid Machine Learning and Time-Series.

Overview Data Section Model Section Stakeholders

Problem Statement

Existing EV market forecasting systems mainly rely on traditional statistical models or standalone ML algorithms that fail to capture complex spatial-temporal relationships, nonlinear market behavior, and dynamic policy impacts. This project develops a hybrid forecasting framework that is accurate, interpretable, scalable, and privacy-preserving.

Objectives

  1. Hybrid CNN-LSTM-Attention deep forecasting for EV registration trends
  2. Robust preprocessing, denoising, and multi-factor feature engineering
  3. Explainable AI with SHAP, LIME, and attention weight heatmaps
  4. Federated learning for cross-city privacy preservation
  5. Physics-informed battery and grid capacity constraints
  6. Rigorous benchmarking vs classical (ARIMA/SARIMA) and machine learning (RF/XGBoost) baselines

Features & Target Variable

Input features engineered across spatial, temporal, and historical lag dimensions:

Year State / UT Vehicle Type Lag-1 Registrations Lag-2 Registrations Rolling 3M Mean Rolling Momentum Incentives / Subsidy Index

Forecast Target: Registrations

Predicts future volume of EV sales and adoption per state and vehicle segment.

Preprocessing & Validation:

Outlier detection, zero-imputation for new market entrants, chronological 80/20 train-test split (avoiding lookahead leakage), and RobustScaler fitted exclusively on training sets.

Proposed

Hybrid CNN-LSTM-Attention

1D-CNN layers extract localized temporal features; Bi-LSTM captures sequential market memory; Attention dynamically focuses on policy inflection points.

Baseline ML

Random Forest & XGBoost

Ensemble decision trees and gradient boosting trained on lagged features, rolling momentum, and categorical spatial encodings.

Statistical

ARIMA / SARIMA / Prophet

Classical univariate and seasonal auto-regressive moving average baselines for linear trend and seasonal decomposition.
